> Q2.5 Before you run any experiment in Testing the Baseline, write down your predictions:
> will model A pass the mirror test? The rotation test? At what speed should it break down,
> given your reach estimate and Figure 1?

**Mirror test.** I expect model A to mostly pass. Figure 1(a) shows that training motion already
covers all directions, so mirrored trajectories are familiar motion. Only the mirrored digit shapes
are new. A ConvLSTM's 3×3 kernels are not constrained to be symmetric, so I do not expect exact
consistency: C_M will be clearly above zero. I do expect it to be small compared with E(5), and
E_M to be only slightly higher than E(5).

**Rotation test.** I also expect model A to mostly pass, but less cleanly than the mirror test.
Rotated directions are equally familiar (θ → θ + 90°). However, a 90° rotation turns the mostly
vertical strokes of MNIST digits into horizontal ones, a bigger change in local appearance than
mirroring. I therefore predict C_R > C_M and E_R > E_M, with neither gap becoming catastrophic.

**Speed.** My reach estimate for model A is 8 px per step along rows and columns (≈11 px along a
diagonal). The typical speed in Figure 1(b) is about 3.3 px/frame, so 2× is about 6.6 px/frame
and 3× about 10 px/frame.

- **At 2×**, most displacements are still inside the reach, so the model should keep tracking the
  digits. Its error will rise noticeably, because it has never seen this speed and will probably
  move the digits too slowly. The fastest digits (about 9 px/frame at 2×) are already beyond 8 px.
- **At 3×**, the typical displacement exceeds the reach, so the model cannot connect a digit's old
  position with its new one in a single update. I expect it to break down here: predictions will
  lag behind and blur into smears, with E(2) at 3× approaching the Blank level.
